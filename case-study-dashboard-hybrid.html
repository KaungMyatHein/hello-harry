01 — The morning ritual

Twelve copy-pastes. Every day.

Mei, the ops manager, did this every single morning before the team's standup.

Source 01Xero

Revenue and expenses, updated daily. Required login — no direct export to the dashboard the team actually reviewed.

Source 02Spreadsheets

Manual compilation: 12 copy-pastes per morning. Each paste introduced opportunities for fat-finger errors no one caught until the weekly review.

Source 03Supplier portal

Payment tracking lived in a separate login. Mei alt-tabbed between three browser windows just to compile one report.

CostStale by 9am

By the time data was compiled, it was already a day behind. The team made decisions on yesterday's numbers — and didn't know it.

What if we could give her those 30 minutes back? And give the team today's numbers?

The fix wasn't more software. It was less software.

04 — The redesign

One screen. Every KPI.

Restructured the IA so the daily ritual collapsed into a glance. Then layered alerts and status onto the surfaces that mattered.

Surface 01Alert banner

Actionable notification for pending amendments at the top of the dashboard. Mei sees state before she even thinks about it.

Surface 02Xero sync status

Color-coded sync indicators per row. Green: synced. Amber: pending. Red: failed. State at a glance, no clicking.

Surface 03Amendment badges

"Need acknowledgement" surfaces per row, not buried in a sub-page. The action is where the data is.

Surface 04Color-coded rows

Visual hierarchy by payment status. The eye lands on what needs work before reading a single number.

Amendment details modal · three key improvements

01 · Original vs. amended side-by-side in context. No mental math, no separate tab.

02 · Item-level changes with price diffs and savings per product. Finance can audit without leaving the page.

03 · One-click acknowledge — confirm without leaving the row. The action is one tap from the data.

06 — Lessons

Four lessons.

Lesson 01

Time savings compound at scale.

0.5h/day sounds small. Across a year, it's 125+ hours — three extra work weeks. Daily friction is the bug that pays compound interest.

Lesson 02

The data existed. The design didn't.

No new APIs needed. The entire ROI came from restructuring information architecture. The bottleneck wasn't engineering — it was attention.

Lesson 03

Reducing tools beats adding features.

3 systems to 1 dashboard. The highest-impact decision was subtraction. Every removed tool removed a context switch — and context switches are where stale data is born.

Lesson 04

Remote research works with the right questions.

Interviewing Mei about her daily routine surfaced both the pain points and the time-cost data we needed for ROI. Method discipline made the business case credible to Finance.
