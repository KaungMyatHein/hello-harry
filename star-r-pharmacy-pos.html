01 · Situation
The pain

A legacy POS was taxing every shift.

Raytail ran two retail operations — a pharmacy and a supermarket — on a legacy Windows POS that required days of cashier training. The workflow tax was costing real money in slow checkout and rising error rates.

Two product surfaces. One ageing system. One designer.

02 · Task
The deliverable

Unify two stores under one touchscreen system.

Redesign the legacy Windows POS into a modern touchscreen system serving both pharmacy and supermarket operations, with zero training dependency for new cashiers.

03 · Action
What I did

Owned the system end-to-end.

  • Owned design direction as the sole UX/UI Designer — 200+ screens across 22 Figma files covering 14 modules.
  • Ran cashier shadowing, supplier interviews, and journey mapping in FigJam and Miro — finding where the legacy flow taxed real users, not where it looked dated.
  • Applied an impact × frequency prioritization on cashier workflows so the highest-leverage tasks shipped first, not the loudest complaints.
  • Validated with Maze unmoderated tests, Hotjar session replays, and A/B tests on the live system — measuring time-on-task as the success metric.
  • Enforced WCAG standards and ran handoff via EightShapes Spec + Jira so engineering shipped what was designed. Mentored a junior designer who joined the team.

05 · Reflection
What I'd do differently

Domain experts are participants, not reviewers.

I underestimated how much pharmacy compliance constraints would reshape the checkout flow — I should have brought the pharmacist into discovery in week 1, not week 6. That single decision would have saved one full design iteration. Lesson — in regulated retail, domain experts are research participants, not stakeholders you review with at the end.
