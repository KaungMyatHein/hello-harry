## Script

Hi, I'm Harry Hein. I'm a senior product designer based in Bangkok.

I started in engineering, then switched to design — because I kept seeing software that made simple things hard. Over the past seven years, I've worked on tools for cashiers, pharmacists, ops managers, and social-commerce sellers, across Myanmar, Singapore, and the Maldives.

The work taught me one thing. Most enterprise software isn't broken because the engineers built it wrong. It's broken because nobody asked the people using it. So I always start there — sitting with cashiers during real shifts, with sellers during their live streams, with ops managers during their morning routine. Then I design what they actually need.

My philosophy is KISS — Keep It Super Simple. I take complicated software and strip it down to the parts that matter. At Raytail in the Maldives, that approach drove 200% ROI on one project, and over 400% on another. I've also built two free tools — an AI usability tester and a Figma plugin for design tokens — used by designer friends around the world.

When I'm not working, I run a UX community in Myanmar, to help junior designers coming up.

I'm bilingual in English and Burmese, and open to senior product design roles starting 2026 — remote globally, or based in Thailand or Singapore.

## Speaker notes
- Warm, conversational — like you're at coffee with a friend, not pitching
- First sentence: settle in, slight smile
- **"I started in engineering, then switched to design"** — say it like a small personal story. This is the origin beat
- **"Because I kept seeing software that made simple things hard"** — slight emphasis on "hard". That's the motivation
- The four user types (cashiers / pharmacists / ops managers / sellers) — say each clearly with a small pause between. These are real people you've worked for
- **"The work taught me one thing."** — slight pause after, like you're about to share something you've thought about for years
- **"Nobody asked the people using it"** — emphasis on "nobody". This is the insight
- "I always start there" — quiet, like a personal commitment
- The three settings (real shifts / live streams / morning routine) — vivid, paint the picture
- **KISS** — spell K-I-S-S clearly, then expand to "Keep It Super Simple" on the next breath
- "200% ROI on one project, and over 400% on another" — say both numbers cleanly
- **"Two free tools"** — slight pride. They're evidence, not claims
- "When I'm not working" — natural pivot to the community beat
- "Junior designers coming up" — slight warmth
- Closing — friendly invitation tone, not announcement
- End with steady eye contact to camera
