10/12

Outputs didn't sound like them. Full AI rewrite eroded voice, fabricated claims, and broke trust on review.
11/12

Between what they wrote and what the JD actually asked for. Gap analysis was guesswork — and guesswork doesn't scale.
12/12

Across every interview: "Show me what's missing. Let me decide what to change." Editorial control was the trust contract.

Three design decisions came directly from these findings: base resume first (give them a structured starting point), gaps before praise (show problems first), and no full AI rewrite (preserve voice and authorship).

The problem isn't the resume. It's the process.
03 — The core bet

Build the engine once.

Treat the resume as a living argument. Every job description is a new brief.

If AI can analyze a JD against the designer's actual resume data — surface gaps, rank by impact, generate rewrite-ready suggestions — the designer gets intelligence instead of guesswork. Build the engine once. Run it for every application.

Options considered · rejected with reasoning

Rejected · 01
Chrome extension. Keyword matching without resume context. Too shallow — pattern matches "leadership" without understanding what the designer actually led.
Rejected · 02
Figma plugin. Wrong context. Resume editing happens in a doc editor, not a canvas tool. The friction of switching apps would kill adoption.
Rejected · 03
Simple checklist. Surfaces what's missing but still requires the designer to do all the cognitive work. Saves no time on the bottleneck.
Chosen · 04
Integrated pipeline. Base resume + JD input + AI gap analysis + ranked suggestions + cover letter. Full context, one environment, editorial control preserved at every stage.
The engine wasn't faster autocomplete. It was a comparator the designer never had before.
04 — The pipeline

Six stages. One source of truth.

Built with Claude (vibe-coded proof of concept), Figma variables synced as tokens, prototyped in Figma Make, tested with 5 designers, iterated with 8 beta users.

  1. Base resume. Split-panel editor with live PDF preview. The single source of truth for every downstream operation.
  2. Paste the JD. Any job board. System normalizes messy copy-paste — bullet artifacts, weird unicode, broken formatting.
  3. AI analyzes. Claude reads the JD against the base resume. Extracts required skills, seniority signals, and culture language.
  4. Gap analysis. Match score + missing keywords + role intelligence — in plain language, ranked by impact.
  5. Suggestions. Specific rewrites ordered by leverage. Apply, skip, or modify. The designer always decides what ships.
  6. Cover letter. JD + resume → three-paragraph draft in under 5 minutes. Editable in-place.

05 — Key design decisions

Four calls. One trust contract.

01 — Foundation

Base resume first.

The analyzer needs real data to measure the gap. Everything downstream depends on having a structured source of truth — not a blank doc and a vague intent.

02 — Inversion

Gaps before praise.

Show problems first. Inverted from instinct — drives action over reassurance. Designers ignored "looks great!" coaching; they acted on "here's what's missing."

03 — Restraint

No full AI rewrite.

Full rewrite fabricates claims and loses the designer's voice. Suggestions + editorial control preserve authorship — and authorship preserves trust.

04 — Honesty

AI badge gone on export.

A working-session marker only. The final deliverable is always clean. Hiring teams don't need to see the editing layer — they need to see the work.

What we got wrong first

V1 showed all suggestions simultaneously — users felt overwhelmed. Added impact ranking so highest-leverage changes surface first.

3/5 testers said the gap analysis felt discouraging. Softened the framing from "missing" to "opportunity to strengthen" without softening the data.

07 — Lessons

What this taught me.

Lesson 01

Intelligence over automation.

Gap analysis + editorial control delivers 80% of the time savings with 100% of the authenticity. Full automation would have killed both.

Lesson 02

The engine model.

Build the base resume once. Every application is a run of that engine — not a blank page. The leverage is in the comparison, not the composition.

Lesson 03

Context changes everything.

The same resume data produces different tailoring for each role. The intelligence isn't in the model — it's in the comparison between resume and JD.

Lesson 04

Trust as UX.

A designer who knows AI can't silently change their resume engages with suggestions more openly. Visible agency is the precondition for trust.
