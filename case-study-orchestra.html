01 — Concept

Design Without
a Client

Most of my work is constrained by business requirements, technical limitations, and stakeholder consensus. Orchestra was a deliberate break — a space to explore visual composition for its own sake.

No deliverable other than: does this feel right?

"Good designers know the rules. Great designers know when the rules are the constraint."

The name came from the idea of multiple elements — type, space, weight — performing in relation to each other. A page is a composition. Every choice affects every other.

02 — Composition

Rhythm, Weight,
and Whitespace

The project focused on three compositional principles I wanted to internalise. Not as theory — as felt practice. I worked through dozens of variations before each one clicked.

Rhythm
Consistent vertical cadence creates readability without explicit grids.
Weight
Contrast in size and tone guides attention without arrows or labels.
Whitespace
Empty space is a design element, not absence of design.
03 — Publishing

The Design IS
the Code

Orchestra was built and published entirely inside Figma Sites — no export, no handoff, no developer. Figma Sites let me publish directly from the canvas, which meant the design intent was preserved 1:1.

No translation loss. What I designed was what shipped.

What worked
  • Direct publish from canvas
  • Zero translation loss in output
  • Instant iteration — change, publish, done
The limits
  • No custom interaction logic
  • Font choices are constrained
  • Can't embed video or rich media
04 — Reflection

What It
Gave Me Back

Side projects without clients teach you what you actually believe about design — not what you've been told to believe. Orchestra reminded me that restraint is a skill.

The best compositions I made were the ones with the fewest elements.

01 — Constraints reveal taste.
Without a brief, your aesthetic defaults are exposed. I discovered I lean toward severe typographic hierarchy and generous negative space. That's useful to know about yourself.
02 — Process without pressure is underrated.
The absence of a deadline produced more considered decisions. Not every project should move fast. Some things only become clear when you're not rushing toward a handoff.
