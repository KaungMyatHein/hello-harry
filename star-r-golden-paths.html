01 · Situation
The pain

Live testing was the bottleneck.

Live usability testing was the bottleneck on every feature — by the time results came back, the design was already in dev, and any uncovered friction meant costly rework.

01b · Research

Late friction was expensive friction.

02 · Task
The deliverable

Surface friction before live testing.

Build a synthetic-persona testing layer that runs hi-fi Figma prototypes against AI personas before live testing — so designers could surface friction patterns and fix them while the design was still cheap to change.

03 · Action
What I did

Heuristics in. Structured friction out.

  • Designed a validation framework that mapped Nielsen heuristics plus custom usability rules into prompt evaluators.
  • Built synthetic personas with the Gemini API, anchored to real archetypes from prior interview data — not invented profiles.
  • Wired the pipeline to Figma prototypes, feeding screen sequences and interaction logs into the personas as input context.
  • Iterated on prompt evaluators until they output structured friction reports — blocker, severity, suggested fix — instead of vibes.
  • Validated the system by comparing its output to subsequent live user testing on the same prototype.

05 · Reflection
What I'd do differently

Affective UX still needs humans.

The 2 blockers I missed were both emotional friction — frustration, hesitation — that the personas couldn't simulate without richer behavioral context. Lesson — synthetic personas are strong on procedural usability but weak on affective UX. The human signal still matters where feelings are the friction.
