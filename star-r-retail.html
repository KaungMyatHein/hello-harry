01 · Situation
The pain

A feedback log without a roadmap.

At Raytail's supermarket POS, cashiers were complaining about slow checkout and rising error rates — but the feedback log was a chaotic mix of "make it look nicer" and "this kills me every shift."

Without prioritization, every fix would be guesswork dressed as a roadmap.

02 · Task
The deliverable

Triage friction into a ranked roadmap.

Optimize cashier workflows by triaging real friction into a ranked roadmap, then ship only the changes that would measurably move time-on-task and error rate.

03 · Action
What I did

Two lenses. Empirical validation.

  • Built a two-lens prioritization method — impact × frequency — that scored every feedback item against task-time and error-rate signals, not gut feel.
  • Ran journey mapping in Miro across 3 cashier archetypes built from real interview data, so each lens had a face attached.
  • Validated hypotheses with Maze unmoderated tests and Hotjar session replays on the live POS — looking at where cashiers stalled, not where they said they stalled.
  • Ran A/B tests on the top-3 ranked changes to confirm time-savings empirically before claiming ROI.
  • Logged every shipped change in Jira tied back to its prioritization score so the framework could be re-evaluated next cycle.

Scoring prioritization.js 
// Two-lens prioritization · impact × frequency
function score(item) {
  const impact =
    item.taskTimeSec +
    item.errorRateBps * 2;     // errors weighted 2×

  const frequency =
    item.occurrencesPerShift;

  return impact * frequency;
}

// → Top 12% of items = 67% of pain

05 · Reflection
What I'd do differently

ROI is only as honest as the boundary.

I scoped the prioritization to cashier-side metrics only and missed a back-office workflow that was cheaper to fix and had higher leverage — I should have pulled Warehouse and Finance feedback into lens 1 from day one. Lesson — ROI calculations are only as honest as the boundary you draw around them.
